Jiru Gutema (born 2004) is an Ethiopian software engineer and full-stack developer based in Addis Ababa. He is currently an undergraduate at Addis Ababa University3, where he is concurrently developing the university's Angular/.NET-based property management system, and a contributor at Mereb Technologies on a Bahmni-based healthcare platform deployed across Ethiopian clinics.2

Jiru is known for several small but widely-installed browser extensions and open-source utilities, including Hide YouTube Shorts and Page Marker for Mozilla Firefox, and the swagger-html command-line tool for bundling OpenAPI specifications into self-contained documents.4 His professional work centers on the seam between front-end and back-end systems, with a stated preference for code that "the next person to read it can reason about without a meeting".5

Outside of his employment, Jiru has contributed patches to OpenMRS, Prisma, Next.js and shadcn/ui.6 He is a finalist of the 2024 INSA Cybersecurity Summer Program capture-the-flag and was treasurer of the Addis Ababa University developer community in 2025.

Early life and education edit

School and bootcamps

Jiru was raised in Addis Ababa and educated in the Ethiopian public school system. In 2022, he enrolled in a Google-backed full-stack web development bootcamp known locally as the Coding Academy, which he has credited as the place where he "first wrote a CRUD app, broke it, and learned why version control exists".5

In the summer of 2024 he completed a six-week intensive in offensive and defensive computer security at the Information Network Security Agency (INSA), covering network forensics, malware analysis and web exploitation, and reached the finals of the program's closing capture-the-flag competition.7

University

Since 2022, Jiru has been pursuing a Bachelor of Science in software engineering at Addis Ababa University, with expected graduation in 2027. His coursework spans algorithms, distributed systems, software architecture and machine learning. He served as treasurer of the campus developer community in the 2024–2025 academic year and led a four-week Linux from Scratch series for first-year students.3

Career edit

"Jiru is the rare junior who reads the codebase before opening the PR. He'll ask the question you should have asked yourself, and his commit messages read like a diary you actually want to read."

Ethioware EdTech (2024)

Jiru's first professional engagement was a backend internship at Ethioware EdTech in 2024, where he contributed to the version 1 quiz engine of an Ethiopian-curriculum tutoring platform. He authored the question-bank importer that, according to internal communications, remained in weekly use by the platform's content team into 2026.9 The work was carried out in Express.js on Node.js with a MongoDB backing store.

Gulit Marketplace (2024–2025)

Between late 2024 and 2025, Jiru served as a backend developer at Gulit Marketplace, a multi-vendor e-commerce platform aimed at local Ethiopian merchants. He designed the order and payment service, built a chat-based order flow used as the platform's primary checkout surface, and implemented a webhook-driven notification pipeline for vendor payouts. The system was built primarily in NestJS on PostgreSQL via Prisma, and deployed in Docker containers.10

Current roles (2025–present)

Since 2025, Jiru has held two concurrent positions. At Addis Ababa University he leads development on a property management system, a single-page Angular front-end backed by a .NET 8 API and a PostgreSQL database using Entity Framework Core; the system tracks university assets across faculties, including inventory, transfers and depreciation reporting.3 He is the principal author of the audit-log module and the Microsoft Excel import pipeline, which replaced a 12,000-row spreadsheet previously maintained by hand.

Concurrently, Jiru works remotely at Mereb Technologies, customising the Bahmni EMR/HMS distribution for Ethiopian clinics. His contributions there include appointment-flow modifications, lab-order forms and clinician-friendly patient summaries, working across both the OpenMRS module layer and the React-based front-end shell.2

Views and influences edit

Jiru has cited Martin Kleppmann's Designing Data-Intensive Applications as a continuing influence on his approach to backend architecture, particularly its treatment of schema evolution and storage trade-offs.16 He has also publicly recommended the Veritasium, 3Blue1Brown, StarTalk Radio and Lex Fridman programmes as informal sources of mathematical and physical intuition.

In a 2026 essay titled "Why I stopped chasing perfect schemas", Jiru argued that, in small-team production settings, "evolvable but slightly embarrassing" database schemas tend to outperform up-front normalisation, provided that migrations are version-controlled and reversible.5 The essay was discussed on Hacker News and reposted in the Postgres Weekly newsletter.17

Reception edit

Colleagues and mentors have described Jiru's work in generally positive terms. Mahlet Bekele, a project manager on the AAU asset-management programme, stated that he "shipped the asset import pipeline two days early and fixed three bugs nobody had reported yet", adding that she had stopped reviewing his database migrations because "they just work".18 Daniel Habte, a mentor from the Coding Academy, characterised him as "the most curious engineer I've mentored", attributing the durability of his code to a habit of "not stopping digging until he understands the why".19 Selam Wolde, chief technology officer of Gulit Marketplace, noted that Jiru was able to take ownership of an existing monorepo within his first week and propose refactors that the team accepted.20

Personal life edit

Jiru is a native speaker of Afaan Oromoo and is fluent in Amharic and English. He has stated that he reads "more than he should" and maintains a personal practice of completing one small weekend programming project each month.5 As of 2026 he resides in Addis Ababa and uses a refurbished 2014 ThinkPad running Linux as his primary development machine.
